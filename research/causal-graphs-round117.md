# Causal graphs — Round 117: three drug classes from one gene, and a function CARD calls "putative"

## MSH2 resists three unrelated classes, and the reason is not written

> *"MSH2 is a **mismatch repair** gene in fungi. **Strains with alterations** in this gene
> **exhibit** the resistance to **Polyenes**…, **Echinocandins**…, and **Azoles**…"*

Every other record in this corpus resists one drug or one class. This one resists **three,
with three different targets**, from a gene whose function is DNA repair.

The obvious reading is **hypermutation** — lose repair, raise the mutation rate, and
resistance follows by any route. CARD does not say it. **That is the most inviting
inference in the corpus after rpoB's rifampicin edge**, and a test bans *hypermut*,
*mutation rate* and *resist* from the config's asserted text while requiring the note to
name the omission.

Also kept: *"strains with alterations **exhibit**"* is an observation about strains, not a
claim about the protein — and the config says so.

## pepQ hedges the function itself

> *"Rv2535c or pepQ encodes a **putative** Xaa-Pro aminopeptidase and is a **new** genetic
> determinant of **low-level** bedaquiline and clofazimine **cross-resistance**…"*

**Four qualifiers in one sentence** — the function is *putative*, the determinant is *new*,
the resistance is *low-level*, and it is *cross-resistance* to two drugs.

The first is a step beyond anything earlier: round 111's **drmA** was *"uncharacterized"*,
meaning its behaviour was unstudied, but its identity as a 6-pass membrane protein was not
in doubt. Here **the assignment itself is uncertain**, so *"putative"* stays in the node
label rather than only in the notes.
