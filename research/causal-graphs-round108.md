# Causal graphs — Round 108: two records one inference from a textbook mechanism

Both of these are a single step away from a story any reader would supply, and **CARD
supplies neither.**

## FUR1

> *"FUR1 is a fungal **uracil phosphoribosyltransferase** … the UPRT protein … **a key
> regulating enzyme in pyrimidine salvage**. Mutations in FUR1 **have been shown to** confer
> resistance to 5-flucytosine."*

5-flucytosine is a prodrug converted by pyrimidine salvage. The activation-loss story is
round 56's pncA shape and it is **standard** — and CARD names the pathway, names the
resistance, and never joins them. So the graph carries the enzyme's pathway role and stops.

The resistance claim is also **attributed**, not asserted: *"have been shown to"*.

## Hmg1

> *"Hmg1 is an **HMG-CoA reductase**-encoding gene with mutations in the gene **causing**
> triazole resistance."*

HMG-CoA reductase is upstream of ergosterol; azoles target ergosterol synthesis. That is
one inference away and **not in the sentence**. The graph asserts the enzyme identity and
nothing else.

Note the verb: *"causing"*, unhedged — stronger than most of this corpus's resistance
claims, which say *"can confer"* or *"contribute to"*. The strength of the claim and the
absence of the mechanism are independent, and both are recorded.

A test bans *flucytosine*/*prodrug*/*activat* from FUR1's asserted text and
*ergosterol*/*azole*/*triazole* from Hmg1's.
