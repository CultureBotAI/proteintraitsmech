# Causal graphs — Round 104: an inconsistency of mine, found by re-reading my own reason

Round 84 read the fungal cytochrome P450 family and left it, recording: *"thinner than
round 66's EF-Tu, which at least had a functional name and a causal 'confer'."*

Put side by side, that does not hold:

| | definition |
|---|---|
| **EF-Tu** (curated, r66) | *"Sequence variants of **elongation factor Tu** that **confer resistance** to different classes of antibiotics."* |
| **P450** (left, r84) | *"Fungal **cytochrome P450 enzymes** which include mutations or other modifications **to confer resistance** to antifungal drug compounds."* |

**Both name a function and claim resistance; neither gives a mechanism.** Round 66 curated
the first on exactly that basis. Leaving the second was an inconsistency, not a standard —
and the reason I wrote at the time cited a distinction that is not there.

## What is asserted

Only the functional identity: *"Fungal cytochrome P450 enzymes"*. A test asserts **both**
configs — EF-Tu's and P450's — contain no *bind*, *inhibit* or *resist* in their asserted
text, so the two stay aligned.

These enzymes are the azole target (lanosterol 14α-demethylase) in most fungi and the
binding story is standard. **That is why the omission needed a test**: it is the most
inviting uncited edge in the family, exactly as #219's promoter arm was.

## The hedges differ between parent and child

* parent: *"mutations **or other modifications**"* — the determinant class is not even
  resolved to mutation
* child: *"mutations"* alone, for triazoles specifically

The child's records were promoted under the parent's config; the difference is recorded in
the config rather than flattened.
